# Global Cancer Structural Inference Framework

A perturbational structural-geometry framework for high-dimensional transcriptomic organization.

This repository contains the analytical pipeline, DuckDB warehouse, reporting system, and emerging AI reasoning framework for the **Global Cancer Structural Inference Framework**.

The project investigates cancer as a systems-level perturbation of transcriptomic organization rather than solely as a collection of differentially expressed genes. Mathematical descriptors are computed first, structural organization is inferred second, and biological interpretation is performed only after the transcriptome has been structurally characterized.

---

## Overview

The Structural Inference Framework seeks to determine whether diverse cancers occupy reproducible regions of transcriptomic structural state space.

Instead of emphasizing individual genes or pathways, the framework integrates complementary mathematical descriptions of transcriptomic organization, including:

* structural complexity
* informational organization
* covariance geometry
* random matrix (Marčenko–Pastur) structure
* nonlinear latent-space geometry
* perturbational stability
* robustness across feature-space perturbations

These independent structural engines are synthesized into coherent structural phenotypes that can subsequently be compared across cancer types and linked to biological annotation systems.

## Computational Architecture

```text
Raw transcriptome
        ↓
Structural descriptors
        ↓
Structural synthesis
        ↓
Structural phenotype
        ↓
Cross-cancer comparison
        ↓
Validation
        ↓
Biological projection
        ↓
Structural Grammar
        ↓
AI-assisted reasoning
        ↓
Hypothesis generation
```

The framework intentionally separates mathematical inference from biological interpretation. AI models are never used to compute structural descriptors; instead, they consume compressed structural evidence generated by the statistical pipeline.

---

## Structural Engines

The framework currently integrates several complementary structural engines.

| Engine                            | Structural Role                                            |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Complexity                        | Organization and concentration of transcriptomic structure |
| Entropy                           | Informational organization of covariance                   |
| Marčenko–Pastur Spectral Analysis | Random versus organized covariance structure               |
| Latent Geometry                   | Nonlinear manifold organization                            |
| Structural Synthesis              | Integration of multiple structural descriptors             |

Each engine contributes an independent perspective on transcriptomic organization. Structural conclusions are based on convergence across engines rather than reliance on any single metric.

## Structural Grammar

A major objective of the project is the development of a formal Structural Grammar.

Rather than generating free-form narrative descriptions, the grammar defines a controlled language capable of translating mathematical structural descriptors into reproducible semantic statements.

The intended workflow is:

```text
Mathematical descriptors
        ↓
Structural Grammar
        ↓
Structural phenotype
        ↓
Controlled interpretation
```

The grammar is being developed independently of biological interpretation and will ultimately serve as the foundation for automated structural reasoning.

## Structural AI Agents (Beta)

Experimental AI agents are now being developed on top of the structural inference framework.

The current proof-of-concept demonstrates the following workflow:

```text
DuckDB warehouse
        ↓
Compressed evidence packets
        ↓
Local language model
        ↓
Structural interpretation
        ↓
Literature questions
        ↓
Hypothesis generation
```

These AI agents perform reasoning only after all statistical inference has been completed. They do not replace the analytical pipeline but provide downstream interpretation and knowledge synthesis.

## DuckDB Warehouse

All analytical results are stored in a centralized DuckDB warehouse.

The warehouse contains:

* structural descriptor tables
* synthesis outputs
* validation results
* robustness analyses
* biological annotation overlays
* reporting views
* AI evidence packets

This architecture separates data generation from reporting and supports reproducible report generation through Quarto.
