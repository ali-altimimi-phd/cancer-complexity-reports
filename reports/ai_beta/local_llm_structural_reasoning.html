Local AI Reasoning Pipeline

Integrating a Local Large Language Model into a Reproducible Scientific Workflow

Purpose

One objective of the Global Cancer Complexity project is to investigate how a local large language model can participate in a reproducible scientific reasoning workflow without replacing statistical inference.

In this architecture, statistical analyses are performed entirely within the R structural inference pipeline and stored in a DuckDB analytical warehouse. The local language model is invoked only after the computational pipeline has completed.

Its responsibility is semantic reasoning: organizing evidence, identifying higher-order biological themes, generating literature questions, and producing structured reasoning artifacts suitable for downstream evidence synthesis.

Pipeline Architecture

Raw transcriptomic data
        ↓
Statistical inference (R)
        ↓
DuckDB analytical warehouse
        ↓
Evidence packets
        ↓
Compressed reasoning briefs
        ↓
Local LLM execution (LM Studio)
        ↓
Reasoning artifacts
        ↓
Targeted literature retrieval
        ↓
Evidence synthesis

Local AI Environment

Component Implementation
Language model Gemma 4 12B QAT
Runtime LM Studio
Execution Local workstation
Prompt generation R
Evidence source DuckDB warehouse
Output Markdown reasoning artifacts

Design Principles

The language model does not perform statistical inference.

All statistical inference—including descriptor calculation, significance testing, quadrant assignment, archetype identification, and structural inference—is completed before the model is invoked.

The local model is responsible only for semantic reasoning.

Specifically, it is instructed to:

  • interpret ontology-specific evidence
  • merge redundant biological concepts
  • distinguish observation from interpretation
  • avoid unsupported causal claims
  • generate targeted literature questions
  • produce structured reasoning artifacts

Demonstration

This demonstration uses the BLAD/TCC comparison.

Independent reasoning briefs were constructed for four biological ontologies:

Ontology Purpose
GO Biological Process Biological programs
GO Molecular Function Molecular activities
KEGG Pathway organization
Hallmark / MSigDB High-level tumor states

Each ontology was submitted independently to the local language model.

GO Biological Process

**THEME NAME:** Epithelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition (EMT) and Cytoskeletal Remodeling
**BLADDER-CANCER INTERPRETATION:** The data strongly suggests a program of structural reorganization characteristic of invasive transitional cell carcinoma. The enrichment of focal adhesion assembly, actin polymerization, and filopodium assembly indicates that the tumor cells are actively remodeling their cytoskeleton to facilitate migration and invasion. The "lost" complexity in extracellular matrix (ECM) organization, coupled with "strongly anti-chaotic" signals in cell adhesion, suggests a transition from a stable epithelial sheet to a more mobile, mesenchymal-like state where cells are actively detaching from the basement membrane and interacting dynamically with the surrounding stroma.
**INCLUDED INDEX ITEMS:** 16, 29, 33, 35, 41, 43, 59, 75, 76, 97
**KEY TERMS:** Cell adhesion, focal adhesion assembly, actin filament polymerization, filopodium assembly, Rho protein signal transduction, ECM organization, angiogenesis.
**STRUCTURAL SIGNAL:** Strong anti-chaotic signals in adhesion and cytoskeleton organization; lost complexity in ECM organization and angiogenesis regulation.
**REDUNDANT / OVERLAPPING TERMS:** 16 (regulation of cell adhesion), 41 (positive regulation of cell adhesion).
**MISLEADING LABEL WARNINGS:** 35 (blood vessel development) and 97 (negative regulation of angiogenesis) should be interpreted as "tumor-associated angiogenesis" rather than physiological development.
**PUBMED SEARCH STRINGS:** "bladder cancer" AND "EMT" AND "actin polymerization"; "transitional cell carcinoma" AND "focal adhesion" AND "migration"; "bladder cancer" AND "Rho GTPase" AND "cytoskeleton".
**PRIORITY:** high

**THEME NAME:** Immune Evasion and Myeloid-Driven Inflammation
**BLADDER-CANCER INTERPRETATION:** There is a clear signature of immune modulation. The "lost" complexity in neutrophil-mediated immunity, contrasted with "gained" complexity in neutrophil chemotaxis and immunoglobulin production, suggests a shift toward a pro-tumorigenic inflammatory environment. In bladder cancer, this often manifests as the recruitment of myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) or neutrophils that facilitate an immunosuppressive niche. The "negative regulation of T cell activation" is a hallmark of the "cold" tumor microenvironment often seen in advanced transitional cell carcinoma.
**INCLUDED INDEX ITEMS:** 17, 56, 70, 71, 98
**KEY TERMS:** Negative regulation of T cell activation, neutrophil mediated immunity, neutrophil chemotaxis, immunoglobulin production, IL-12 production.
**STRUCTURAL SIGNAL:** Lost complexity in general immunity; gained complexity in chemotaxis and effector production.
**REDUNDANT / OVERLAPPING TERMS:** 56 (neutrophil mediated immunity), 98 (positive regulation of neutrophil chemotaxis).
**MISLEADING LABEL WARNINGS:** 71 (negative regulation of IL-12) may be a proxy for a broader suppression of Th1-type immune responses.
**PUBMED SEARCH STRINGS:** "bladder cancer" AND "neutrophil chemotaxis" AND "immune evasion"; "transitional cell carcinoma" AND "T cell suppression"; "bladder cancer" AND "myeloid-derived suppressor cells".
**PRIORITY:** high

**THEME NAME:** Metabolic Reprogramming and Stress Adaptation
**BLADDER-CANCER INTERPRETATION:** The tumor appears to be undergoing metabolic adaptation to the hypoxic and nutrient-poor conditions of the bladder tumor microenvironment. The inclusion of fatty acid metabolism, retinol metabolism, and response to nutrients suggests a shift toward lipid-based signaling and energy production. The "lost" glucose import (85) is an interesting observation that may indicate a specific metabolic bypass or a shift toward alternative carbon sources (like fatty acids) to maintain biomass. The DNA damage response (51) suggests the presence of ongoing genomic instability or a response to the metabolic stress of the tumor state.
**INCLUDED INDEX ITEMS:** 7, 14, 15, 23, 51, 83, 85
**KEY TERMS:** Fatty acid metabolic process, retinol metabolic process, response to nutrient, DNA damage response, glucose import.
**STRUCTURAL SIGNAL:** Gained complexity in DNA damage response; lost complexity in glucose import.
**REDUNDANT / OVERLAPPING TERMS:** 14 (lipopolysaccharide-mediated signaling) and 31 (cellular response to forskolin) may overlap as lipid-mediated signaling.
**MISLEADING LABEL WARNINGS:** 85 (glucose import) should be interpreted as a potential metabolic shift rather than a simple lack of energy.
**PUBMED SEARCH STRINGS:** "bladder cancer" AND "fatty acid metabolism"; "transitional cell carcinoma" AND "metabolic reprogramming"; "bladder cancer" AND "DNA damage response".
**PRIORITY:** medium

**THEME NAME:** De-differentiation and Loss of Epithelial Identity
**BLADDER-CANCER INTERPRETATION:** This theme captures the loss of normal bladder epithelial characteristics. The "lost" complexity in keratinocyte differentiation and proliferation, combined with the "lost" complexity in the G1/S transition, suggests a state of de-differentiation. Notably, the presence of "neuron" related terms (synaptic signaling, neuron projection) in a bladder cancer context is a classic "misleading label" often representing neuroendocrine differentiation or the activation of primitive, non-epithelial developmental programs that the tumor co-opts for survival.
**INCLUDED INDEX ITEMS:** 12, 20, 28, 30, 66, 69, 78, 80
**KEY TERMS:** Keratinocyte differentiation, G1/S transition, centrosome cycle, neuron differentiation (proxy for de-differentiation).
**STRUCTURAL SIGNAL:** Lost complexity in differentiation and cell cycle transition.
**REDUNDANT / OVERLAPPING TERMS:** 12 (neuronal synaptic plasticity), 22 (synaptic signaling), 28 (neuron projection development), 66 (neuron differentiation), 80 (CNS development).
**MISLEADING LABEL WARNINGS:** All "neuron" and "synaptic" terms are likely proxies for neuroendocrine differentiation or "lineage infidelity" in the bladder cancer cells; they do not imply the formation of neurons.
**PUBMED SEARCH STRINGS:** "bladder cancer" AND "de-differentiation"; "transitional cell carcinoma" AND "neuroendocrine differentiation"; "bladder cancer" AND "keratinocyte differentiation loss".
**PRIORITY:** medium

GO Molecular Function

**THEME NAME:** TGF-β/BMP Signaling and Kinase Cascade Activation
**BLADDER-CANCER INTERPRETATION:** The presence of SMAD binding, BMP receptor activity, and multiple classes of protein kinase activity (tyrosine and serine/threonine) strongly suggests the activation of the TGF-β/BMP signaling axis. In bladder cancer, TGF-β is a well-characterized driver of the epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), chemoresistance, and metastasis. The convergence of SMAD binding with receptor-specific activity (BMP/TGF-β) indicates a functional capacity for SMAD-mediated transcriptional regulation, a hallmark of urothelial progression.
**INCLUDED INDEX ITEMS:** 11, 24, 26, 27, 36, 39, 45, 46
**KEY TERMS:** SMAD binding, BMP receptor activity, protein tyrosine kinase activity, transmembrane receptor kinase activity.
**STRUCTURAL SIGNAL:** Convergence of SMAD-specific binding with multiple receptor-type kinase activities (Tyrosine and Serine/Threonine).
**REDUNDANT / OVERLAPPING TERMS:** 26 (protein tyrosine kinase), 27 (protein kinase), 36 (transmembrane receptor protein tyrosine kinase), 39 (kinase activity).
**MISLEADING LABEL WARNINGS:** "Kinase activity" is a broad category; its significance here is derived from its co-occurrence with specific SMAD and BMP receptor terms.
**PUBMED SEARCH STRINGS:** "TGF-beta signaling bladder cancer SMAD binding", "BMP receptor activity urothelial carcinoma", "tyrosine kinase activation bladder cancer".
**PRIORITY:** high

**THEME NAME:** Epigenetic Remodeling and Transcriptional Regulation
**BLADDER-CANCER INTERPRETATION:** The enrichment of histone acetyltransferase (HAT) binding, histone deacetylase (HDAC) binding, and sequence-specific DNA binding suggests a high capacity for epigenetic reprogramming. Bladder cancer often utilizes HDACs and HATs to modulate the expression of tumor suppressor genes and pro-metastatic factors. The inclusion of nuclear receptor binding further supports a program of coordinated transcriptional control.
**INCLUDED INDEX ITEMS:** 54, 58, 63, 64
**KEY TERMS:** Histone acetyltransferase binding, histone deacetylase binding, sequence-specific DNA binding, nuclear receptor binding.
**STRUCTURAL SIGNAL:** Simultaneous enrichment of both "gain" (HAT) and "loss" (HDAC, DNA binding) complexity engines in chromatin-modifying terms.
**REDUNDANT / OVERLAPPING TERMS:** None.
**MISLEADING LABEL WARNINGS:** "Nuclear receptor binding" can be broad; it may represent a specific hormone receptor or a general transcription factor class.
**PUBMED SEARCH STRINGS:** "Histone acetyltransferase bladder cancer", "HDAC activity transitional cell carcinoma", "epigenetic remodeling bladder cancer".
**PRIORITY:** high

**THEME NAME:** Protein Modification and Proteostasis (Palmitoylation & Ubiquitination)
**BLADDER-CANCER INTERPRETATION:** The data highlights specific capacities for protein palmitoylation (S-palmitoyltransferase) and ubiquitin-related interactions. Palmitoylation is critical for the membrane localization of signaling proteins, while ubiquitin ligase binding suggests active regulation of protein degradation pathways (e.g., degradation of cyclins or p53). This suggests a system focused on fine-tuning the stability and localization of signaling effectors.
**INCLUDED INDEX ITEMS:** 3, 6, 44, 60
**KEY TERMS:** Palmitoyltransferase activity, ubiquitin conjugating enzyme binding, ubiquitin protein ligase binding.
**STRUCTURAL SIGNAL:** Specificity of protein-cysteine S-palmitoyltransferase activity combined with ubiquitin ligase binding.
**REDUNDANT / OVERLAPPING TERMS:** 3 (palmitoyltransferase) and 6 (protein-cysteine S-palmitoyltransferase).
**MISLEADING LABEL WARNINGS:** "Ubiquitin conjugating enzyme binding" is a component of the broader ubiquitin-proteasome system.
**PUBMED SEARCH STRINGS:** "Protein palmitoylation bladder cancer", "ubiquitin ligase activity urothelial carcinoma", "S-palmitoyltransferase bladder cancer".
**PRIORITY:** medium

**THEME NAME:** Transmembrane Transport and Metabolic Flux
**BLADDER-CANCER INTERPRETATION:** The enrichment of D-glucose transmembrane transporters, cation transporters, and antiporters suggests a capacity for altered nutrient uptake and ion homeostasis. In the context of bladder cancer, glucose transporter activity is often associated with the Warburg effect (aerobic glycolysis), while cation transporters may relate to the maintenance of the electrochemical gradient in the urothelium or the regulation of cell volume.
**INCLUDED INDEX ITEMS:** 40, 50, 100, 101
**KEY TERMS:** D-glucose transmembrane transporter activity, ATPase-coupled cation transmembrane transporter activity, antiporter activity.
**STRUCTURAL SIGNAL:** Convergence of specific glucose transport with broader cation and antiporter transport.
**REDUNDANT / OVERLAPPING TERMS:** 50 (protein transmembrane transporter) is a broad parent term for 100 and 101.
**MISLEADING LABEL WARNINGS:** "Antiporter activity" is non-specific; its relevance depends on the specific ions being transported.
**PUBMED SEARCH STRINGS:** "Glucose transporter bladder cancer", "cation transporter activity urothelial carcinoma", "transmembrane transport bladder cancer".
**PRIORITY:** medium

**THEME NAME:** Cytoskeletal Dynamics and ECM Interaction
**BLADDER-CANCER INTERPRETATION:** The presence of beta-tubulin binding, vinculin binding, and hyaluronic acid binding suggests a program related to structural integrity, cell migration, and interaction with the extracellular matrix (ECM). Hyaluronic acid is a major component of the bladder ECM and is frequently upregulated in invasive transitional cell carcinoma, facilitating cell motility and tissue remodeling.
**INCLUDED INDEX ITEMS:** 32, 52, 77, 99
**KEY TERMS:** Beta-tubulin binding, vinculin binding, hyaluronic acid binding, dystroglycan binding.
**STRUCTURAL SIGNAL:** Co-occurrence of microtubule-associated proteins (beta-tubulin) and focal adhesion/ECM-associated proteins (vinculin, hyaluronic acid).
**REDUNDANT / OVERLAPPING TERMS:** None.
**MISLEADING LABEL WARNINGS:** "Dystroglycan binding" is often associated with muscle, but can also appear in other tissues; its relevance here may be secondary to the ECM/migration theme.
**PUBMED SEARCH STRINGS:** "Hyaluronic acid bladder cancer", "vinculin expression transitional cell carcinoma", "beta-tubulin bladder cancer".
**PRIORITY:** medium

**THEME NAME:** RNA Processing and Translation Regulation
**BLADDER-CANCER INTERPRETATION:** The enrichment of mRNA binding and mRNA 5'-UTR binding suggests a capacity for post-transcriptional regulation. This may involve the sequestration of transcripts or the regulation of translation initiation, potentially allowing the cancer cell to rapidly shift its proteome in response to environmental cues.
**INCLUDED INDEX ITEMS:** 53, 93, 90
**KEY TERMS:** mRNA binding, mRNA 5'-UTR binding, translation repressor activity.
**STRUCTURAL SIGNAL:** Convergence of mRNA binding with translation repressor activity.
**REDUNDANT / OVERLAPPING TERMS:** 53 (mRNA 5'-UTR binding) and 93 (mRNA binding).
**MISLEADING LABEL WARNINGS:** "Translation repressor activity" is a functional outcome; the binding terms describe the mechanism.

**PRIORITY:** low

KEGG

**THEME NAME: ECM Remodeling, Cell Adhesion, and Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition (EMT)**
**BLADDER-CANCER INTERPRETATION:** These results suggest a significant structural reorganization of the urothelial barrier and the surrounding stroma. In transitional cell carcinoma (TCC), the loss of epithelial integrity (Tight Junctions) and the activation of Focal Adhesion and ECM-receptor interactions are hallmarks of invasion. The inclusion of vascular smooth muscle contraction signals may indicate a shift toward a mesenchymal phenotype, where the cancer cells acquire contractile properties and remodel the extracellular matrix to facilitate local invasion and metastasis.
**INCLUDED INDEX ITEMS:** 10, 61, 111, 125
**KEY TERMS:** ECM-receptor interaction, Tight junction, Focal adhesion, Vascular smooth muscle contraction.
**STRUCTURAL SIGNAL:** Transition from stable epithelial architecture to a migratory, invasive mesenchymal state.
**REDUNDANT / OVERLAPPING TERMS:** 10, 111 (Adhesion/ECM)
**MISLEADING LABEL WARNINGS:** 125 (Vascular smooth muscle contraction) – Likely represents cytoskeletal remodeling and contractility associated with EMT rather than actual vascular contraction.
**PUBMED SEARCH STRINGS:** "bladder cancer" AND "EMT" AND "ECM remodeling"; "transitional cell carcinoma" AND "focal adhesion" AND "invasion"; "urothelial cell" AND "tight junction" AND "loss of polarity"
**PRIORITY:** high

This may reflect the recruitment of myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) or the activation of the inflammasome.

Hallmark / MSigDB

THEME NAME: Mesenchymal Lineage & Stromal Remodeling
BLADDER-CANCER INTERPRETATION: The co-occurrence of adipogenesis and myogenesis programs strongly suggests a "mesenchymal" transcriptional state. In the context of transitional cell carcinoma (TCC), this likely represents one of two biological phenomena: either the induction of an Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition (EMT) program within the tumor cells themselves, or a highly reactive, desmoplastic strophic environment. This signature is often associated with increased invasive potential and the recruitment of myofibroblasts in the bladder tumor microenvironment.
INCLUDED INDEX ITEMS: 120. HALLMARK_ADIPOGENESIS, 121. HALLMARK_MYOGENESIS
KEY TERMS: EMT, Mesenchymal identity, Desmoplasia, Fibroblast activation, Stromal remodeling.
STRUCTURAL SIGNAL: Mesenchymal differentiation / Tissue remodeling.
REDUNDANT / OVERLAPPING TERMS: ADIPOGENESIS and MYOGENESIS are frequently co-regulated in mesenchymal stem cell and fibroblast lineages.
MISLEADING LABEL WARNINGS: These are "differentiation" markers; in a malignancy, they often signify "dedifferentiation" or "transdifferentiation" rather than functional tissue formation.
PUBMED SEARCH STRINGS: "bladder cancer EMT mesenchymal markers", "adipogenesis myogenesis bladder cancer stroma", "transitional cell carcinoma desmoplasia".
PRIORITY: high

Heme metabolism is critical for iron homeostasis and managing oxidative stress, which are frequently altered in bladder cancer. PRIORITY: low

Observations

Several features are noteworthy.

  • Each ontology was interpreted independently.
  • Similar biological themes emerged across multiple ontologies.
  • The model consolidated redundant enrichment terms into coherent biological programs.
  • Misleading ontology labels were identified and interpreted appropriately.
  • Targeted PubMed search strategies were generated automatically.

The convergence of independent ontology-specific analyses suggests that the reasoning pipeline is capable of identifying higher-order biological themes rather than simply restating individual enrichment terms.

Engineering Perspective

From a software engineering perspective, this demonstration illustrates a modular AI architecture.

The workflow separates:

  • statistical inference
  • evidence management
  • prompt engineering
  • semantic reasoning
  • downstream evidence synthesis

Each stage produces persistent artifacts that can be independently regenerated, inspected, validated, or replaced.

Because the reasoning stage consumes standardized evidence briefs, alternative local or cloud-based language models can be evaluated without modifying the upstream computational pipeline.

Future Development

Current work focuses on biological reasoning.

The next stage of the project extends the same architecture to structural reasoning.

The planned Structural AI Agent will consume Structural Grammar evidence rather than biological ontologies, enabling independent interpretation of transcriptomic geometry, covariance organization, latent-space structure, and systems-level organization before integration with the biological reasoning pipeline.

Summary

This demonstration shows that local language models can be incorporated into a reproducible scientific workflow without replacing conventional statistical analysis.

The resulting architecture combines deterministic computation with controlled semantic reasoning, providing a foundation for AI-assisted evidence synthesis within the broader Global Cancer Complexity framework.
