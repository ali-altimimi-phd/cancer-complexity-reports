3  Second-Order Stability Geometry

Concordance, robustness, trajectories, and boundary structure

4 Purpose

This chapter introduces second-order structural geometry.

Second-order geometry asks:

Are the first-order structural locations stable, recurrent, boundary-sensitive, or representation-dependent?

Where first-order geometry maps the location of tumor systems in structural state space, second-order geometry evaluates the stability and reproducibility of those locations across chips, filter regimes, engines, and quadrant boundaries.

5 Conceptual Scope

Second-order geometry includes:

  • quadrant stability,
  • boundary distance,
  • robustness class,
  • filter-regime trajectory length,
  • chip reproducibility,
  • engine agreement,
  • engine discordance,
  • and structural archetype persistence.

13 Interpretation

Second-order geometry represents the stability structure of the first-order perturbational map.

The aim is not to create new biological categories from a single plot, but to determine which structural positions persist across representational perturbations and which remain boundary-sensitive, unstable, or engine-discordant.
