2  First-Order Perturbational Geometry

Direct structural projections of normal–tumor transcriptomic reorganization

3 Purpose

This chapter introduces the first-order structural geometry of the Global Cancer Structural Inference Framework.

First-order geometry asks:

Where does each normal–tumor comparison lie in structural state space?

Each point in the following plots represents a normal–tumor comparison under a specific chip and filter-regime condition. The axes are not genes, pathways, or PCA components. They are structural descriptors derived from the complexity, entropy, MP spectral, and latent-geometry engines.

The goal is to visualize malignancy as an organizational perturbation of transcriptomic structure.

11 Interpretation

First-order geometry provides the primary visual grammar for the framework.

The central interpretive question is not whether a tumor has a single altered gene or pathway, but whether the tumor system occupies a reproducible location in transcriptomic structural state space.

These plots establish the observed structural positions. Subsequent second-order geometry evaluates whether those positions are concordant, robust, boundary-sensitive, or archetypally stable.
