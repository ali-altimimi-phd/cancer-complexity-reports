21  Structural Archetype Atlas

Recurring Structural Phenotypes Across Cancer Comparisons

22 Purpose

The previous chapter introduced the structural phenotype as the fundamental analytical unit of cross-cancer synthesis. Each cancer comparison is represented by a multidimensional phenotype describing transcriptomic organization, stability, and structural behavior.

The present atlas asks whether recurring classes of structural phenotypes emerge across cancers.

Structural archetypes represent higher-order groupings of structural phenotypes that share similar organizational characteristics despite differences in tissue origin, developmental lineage, and molecular composition.

23 From Structural Phenotypes to Structural Archetypes

A structural phenotype describes the organizational state of an individual cancer comparison. An archetype represents a recurring class of structural phenotypes observed across multiple comparisons.

Structural archetypes therefore occupy a higher level within the synthesis hierarchy.

An archetype summarizes the dominant quadrant behavior, the stability of that behavior across chips and filter regimes, and the confidence of the assignment.

If multiple cancers share an archetype, the result suggests that malignant transcriptomes may converge upon common structural phenotypes despite differences in tissue origin.

The following sections summarize observed archetype assignments and their recurrence across cancer comparisons.

25 Structural Meaning of the Major Archetypes

25.1 Stable QI

  • Persistent Quadrant I occupancy
  • Cross-chip and cross-filter stability
  • Strong evidence for a recurring structural regime
  • Suggests robust transcriptomic reorganization

25.2 Majority QI

  • Predominantly Quadrant I
  • Minor assignment variability
  • Indicates a similar organizational tendency with reduced stability

25.3 Available Stable QI

  • Quadrant I occupancy among available assignments
  • Stability is observed within incomplete chip or filter coverage
  • Suggests a QI-like organizational regime with reduced assignment completeness
  • Should be interpreted cautiously relative to fully cross-chip/cross-filter stable assignments

25.4 Stable QII

  • Persistent Quadrant II occupancy
  • Represents a distinct organizational regime relative to the dominant QI group

25.5 Majority QIV

  • Predominantly Quadrant IV occupancy
  • Indicates a recurring tendency toward an alternative structural regime
  • Suggests that not all cancers converge upon the dominant QI-associated pattern
  • May represent a distinct structural solution rather than instability

25.6 Unstable Mixed

  • No strongly dominant organizational state
  • May indicate proximity to structural boundaries
  • May reflect biological heterogeneity or perturbational sensitivity

26 Archetype Recurrence

Several archetypes recur across multiple cancer comparisons. The most common assignments are variants of Quadrant I organization, including stable_QI, majority_QI, and available_stable_QI. This observation suggests that many cancers may converge upon related organizational states despite substantial differences in tissue origin.

Conversely, stable_QII, majority_QIV, and unstable_mixed archetypes occur less frequently and may represent alternative structural solutions or transitional organizational states.

27 Assignment Stability and Confidence

The confidence plot separates two related but distinct features of archetype assignment: the categorical confidence call and the modal quadrant fraction. Comparisons with high or moderate confidence and large modal quadrant fractions provide stronger evidence for stable archetype membership. Low-confidence or unstable assignments should be treated as weaker evidence, even when a modal quadrant is identifiable.

28 Interpretation and Limitations

Structural archetypes should be interpreted as organizational states rather than molecular categories.

A recurrent archetype does not imply shared genes, mutations, pathways, or cellular origins. Instead, it suggests that distinct cancers may exhibit similar patterns of transcriptomic organization.

Because archetype assignments are derived from structural phenotypes, they summarize multiple descriptor domains simultaneously, including dimensional organization, covariance architecture, entropy structure, latent geometry, and robustness.

Archetypes therefore represent higher-order organizational summaries rather than individual biological mechanisms.

High-confidence recurrent archetypes support the possibility of shared malignant system states. Low-confidence or unstable archetypes suggest either biological heterogeneity, sensitivity to feature selection, or proximity to structural boundaries.

29 Key Observation

The dominant result of the archetype atlas is the recurrence of Quadrant I–associated archetypes across a substantial fraction of cancer comparisons.

Although the specific molecular mechanisms differ among tumors, many cancers appear to occupy related regions of structural phenotype space.

This observation supports the concept that recurring transcriptomic organizational states may exist across malignancies and motivates the more detailed analyses presented in the subsequent atlas chapters.
