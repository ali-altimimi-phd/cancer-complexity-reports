Descriptor Landscape Atlas

Signed Structural Shifts Across Cancer Comparisons

Purpose

The descriptor landscape atlas compares tumor-reference shifts across structural descriptor families. This page asks which structural changes recur across cancers and which comparisons diverge from the common pattern.

The focus is on signed deltas: tumor value minus reference value. Positive and negative shifts are interpreted biologically according to each descriptor family.

From Quadrant Convergence to Descriptor-Level Evidence

The previous atlas showed that many cancer comparisons occupy Quadrant I as their dominant structural regime. The present atlas examines the descriptor-level shifts underlying that convergence.

Here, convergence is evaluated not by quadrant labels alone, but by the recurrence of signed structural changes across independent cancer comparisons. If Quadrant I dominance reflects a genuine shared organizational regime, then related patterns should be visible across the underlying descriptor families.

The heatmap below therefore asks whether cancers move through descriptor space in coordinated ways: toward greater dimensionality, altered entropy structure, redistributed covariance organization, or latent-space separation.

Cross-Cancer Descriptor Landscape

The descriptor landscape provides the first direct descriptor-level view of the Quadrant I convergence pattern. Many cancer comparisons show similar signed shifts across entropy, participation-ratio, and covariance-organization descriptors. This suggests that the dominance of Quadrant I in the previous atlas is not merely a categorical artifact; it reflects coordinated movement across multiple structural descriptor families.

Interpretation and Limitations

Descriptor landscapes provide the most direct view of the structural changes underlying cancer-associated transcriptomic reorganization.

Recurrent positive shifts in dimensionality-related descriptors suggest expansion or redistribution of transcriptomic organization. Recurrent changes in entropy and participation-ratio measures suggest altered allocation of variance across covariance modes. Changes in excess spectral mass and leading-eigenvalue metrics indicate modifications in the concentration of organized covariance structure.

Importantly, the descriptor landscape provides the first descriptor-level evidence underlying the Quadrant I convergence pattern observed in the previous atlas. Similar directional shifts across multiple descriptor families suggest that common structural regimes may emerge despite substantial differences in tissue origin and molecular composition.

Several limitations should nevertheless be considered. Descriptor similarity does not imply shared genes, pathways, mutations, or cellular origins. Different biological mechanisms may generate similar structural outcomes. Furthermore, descriptor-level convergence does not require identical magnitudes of change; cancers may follow related structural trajectories while occupying different locations within descriptor space.

Key Observation

The dominant finding of the Descriptor Landscape Atlas is the recurrence of similar directional shifts across multiple structural descriptor families.

These recurring patterns provide descriptor-level support for the Quadrant I convergence observed in the previous atlas and suggest that many cancers traverse transcriptomic descriptor space along related organizational trajectories.

The next atlas examines whether these recurring structural trajectories are accompanied by shared patterns of stability and robustness.
